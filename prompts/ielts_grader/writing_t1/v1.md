Task Achievement (TA): does the letter achieve its purpose? It must cover every bullet point
  of the task, give each enough detail, and keep a tone and register (formal, semi-formal or
  personal) that fits the person it is written to and the reason for writing.
  4 = the purpose is unclear or some bullets are missed, tone often wrong; 5 = covers the
  bullets unevenly or thinly, tone not always consistent; 6 = covers every bullet, some with
  little detail, tone mostly suitable; 7 = covers every bullet clearly, the purpose is clear
  and the tone is consistent; 8 = every bullet is well developed and the tone is right
  throughout; 9 = fully satisfies every requirement.
Coherence and Cohesion (CC): is the letter easy to follow, with an opening, a logical order
  of points, sensible paragraphs and a close, and linking that helps rather than gets in the
  way?
  4 = ideas hard to follow, linking mechanical or faulty; 5 = some organisation, paragraphing
  or linking weak; 6 = clear overall order, cohesion sometimes faulty or mechanical; 7 =
  logical order, a range of linking used mostly well; 8 = ideas sequenced so the reader
  notices nothing awkward; 9 = effortless flow.
Lexical Resource (LR): range, precision and naturalness of vocabulary, including spelling
  and word formation, and whether the wording suits the register of a letter.
  4 = very limited, frequent errors that strain meaning; 5 = limited range, noticeable
  errors; 6 = adequate range, some imprecision, errors rarely impede meaning; 7 = good range
  with some less common items, occasional slips; 8 = wide, precise, natural; 9 = full
  flexibility and precision.
Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA): variety of sentence structures and how accurate
  they are, including punctuation.
  4 = mostly simple sentences, frequent errors; 5 = limited range of complex forms, errors
  frequent but meaning mostly clear; 6 = mix of simple and complex forms, errors that rarely
  impede communication; 7 = frequent error-free sentences, good control of varied
  structures; 8 = most sentences error-free; 9 = full range, virtually no errors.

How to score:
- Before choosing a band, find the evidence: tick off each bullet point of the task and note
  how much detail the letter gives for it, check that the greeting, the sign-off and the tone
  agree with each other and with who the reader is, and count the clear errors in two or
  three sentences.
- Use the whole scale. Do not drift to 6 or 6.5 by default. A letter that skips a bullet,
  keeps the wrong tone or is full of errors is a 4 or 5; reserve 7 and above for letters that
  earn it on that criterion. The four criteria can differ by a whole band or more.
- Be calibrated, not generous: most real candidate letters fall between bands 4.5 and 7.5, and
  8 or above is for letters that are clearly excellent on that criterion.
- A letter that does not answer the situation in the task cannot score above 4 on Task
  Achievement, however fluent it is.
